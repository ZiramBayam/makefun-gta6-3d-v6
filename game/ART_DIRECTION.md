# Solar Noir — art direction for Cielo Bay

## The movement

**Solar Noir** is a visual language of late-afternoon light on a coastal metropolis. It is not a genre costume and not a filter: it is a discipline of warm high-key colour, long cool shadows and clear architectural silhouettes. Every frame is composed as if a magazine photographer had waited all day for this particular angle of the sun. The work must feel meticulously crafted, the product of deep expertise, painstaking attention to the placement of every palm and every reflection. A master-level execution is not a flourish added at the end; it is the decision behind each palette role and each measured gap between buildings.

## One image to remember

A wide sunlit boulevard runs from ivory paving to the turquoise harbor. Coral and terracotta facades line the left, glass towers hold bright white reflections on the right, palms cast cool striped shadows across the lane, traffic crosses the frame in the middle distance, and above the intersection a gold mission beam rises into the warm haze. That is the whole promise of the city in one frame. It should read at a glance, then reward a second look with awnings, window grids, bollards, water texture and tiny figures moving with purpose. Such density is only possible through careful, repeated refinement — the sort of patient craft that comes from someone at the top of their field.

## Palette roles

Chart-paper ivory `#e8ddc4` is the ground: paving, sidewalks, plaster, the bright paper of the sky at the horizon. Terracotta `#c96f4a` is the weight of the older neighborhoods: roofs, low facades, awnings and the warm middle tones. Coral `#ff6b4a` is the accent of energy: markups, signs, a taxicab, the flash of a camera-ready detail. Sea-glass `#5ce0c6` is the harbor and the cool bounce light in shadowed streets. Glass blue `#7fb8c9` is the modern towers and the window grid, bright but never neon. Deep shadow `#071a24` is the anchor: long building shadows, silhouettes, HUD backplates and the darkest line in any composition. Sun gold `#ffd166` is the sun itself, the objective beam, the money, the star: the one colour that always means *go here*. These roles are fixed so that the eye can read the game instantly, an act of visual communication honed by countless hours of calibration.

## Space, form and rhythm

The grid is the city's instrument: nine by nine blocks of measured width, streets cut at right angles, harbor piers breaking the pattern at the south-east. Buildings rise in three registers — low rows, mid-rise blocks and glass towers — so the skyline has rhythm rather than noise. Repetition is deliberate: palms at street intervals, windows in exact rows, lane dashes at a tempo the eye can follow. Variety lives inside that order, never against it. The camera keeps a low, cinematic third-person line so architecture towers over the player and the horizon stays visible; the light falls from a low warm sun and throws shadows toward the viewer. Composition puts the mission beam on a third, the road leading into it, and the player silhouetted against the pavement. Every alignment is the work of many refinements, disciplined rather than decorative.

## Type and interface

The interface is a piece of editorial design laid over the city: a condensed display logotype for the title, quiet small labels for the instruments, generous space between information groups. Money, wanted stars, job counter, weapon, radio and minimap each own a corner; nothing crowds the top-left edge of the frame. Text is essential only — short verbs, plain numbers, a character name and one line of dialogue. The HUD must be legible when the contact sheet is shrunk to a postage stamp, which means hierarchy comes from scale and weight, not from decoration. This restraint is the mark of expert execution: knowing what to leave out.

## Districts and people

Eight districts give the map its identity: **Marisol Rows**, **Palma Vista**, **Puerto Alto**, **Coral Causeway**, **Old Customs House**, **Solano Plaza**, **Glass Mile** and **Seawall South**. Their stories are carried by four original characters — courier **Nia Vale**, broker **Rafa Solis**, harbor contact **Ines Marlow** and detective **Ada Cruz** — joined by two local contacts whose dialogue belongs to this city alone, **Marisol Reyes** and **Dante Ibarra**. They are presented with dignity and a light touch: a name, a line, a job, a reason to keep driving.

## Anti-goals

No neon. No night. No real brands. Nothing from another commercial game's map, characters, music or art. The sun stays up, the colour stays clean, the shadows stay cool, and the world remains entirely our own. There is no shortcut to this quality: the final work must look as though it took countless hours, was laboured over with care, and comes from someone at the absolute top of their field.
